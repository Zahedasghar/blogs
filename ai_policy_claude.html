Pakistan National AI Policy: Executive Strategic Brief

Transforming Vision into Action

Executive Summary

Pakistan’s National AI Policy represents a crucial first step in the nation’s digital transformation journey. While the policy demonstrates comprehensive vision across four strategic pillars, it currently lacks the enforceable mechanisms and operational clarity needed for successful implementation. This brief outlines the 10 most critical actions required to transform this framework into a competitive advantage for Pakistan in the global AI economy.

Bottom Line: Without immediate action on risk classification, regulatory authority, and safety standards, Pakistan risks falling further behind regional competitors and missing the AI revolution’s economic benefits.

🎯 Critical Action Items for Immediate Implementation

1. Establish Risk-Based AI Classification System

Timeline: 6 months | Owner: MoITT/Parliament - Implement 4-tier framework: Prohibited, High-Risk, Limited-Risk, Minimal-Risk - Define clear obligations for each tier - Align with EU AI Act and Singapore’s Model AI Governance Framework

2. Create Independent National AI Authority

Timeline: 9 months | Owner: Cabinet/Parliament - Separate from data protection commission - Grant investigation, licensing, and sanction powers - Establish single-window AI registry - Annual budget: PKR 500M minimum

3. Mandate Safety Testing & Evaluation Protocol

Timeline: 12 months | Owner: National AI Authority - Pre-deployment evaluation requirements for high-risk systems - Red-teaming and bias testing standards - 72-hour incident reporting requirement - Certify 3+ testing laboratories

🚨 Five Highest-Impact Gaps Requiring Urgent Action

Gap 1: No Enforceable Obligations

Current State: Policy expresses intent without binding requirements
Risk: Non-compliance at scale, no accountability
Solution: Convert policy intentions into legally binding regulations with clear penalties

Gap 2: Fragmented Governance Structure

Current State: AI Directorate under data protection commission
Risk: Overlapping mandates, slow decision-making
Solution: Establish independent National AI Authority with clear mandate

Gap 3: Unrealistic & Unfunded Targets

Current State: 1M+ training targets without budget allocation
Risk: Credibility loss, missed deadlines
Solution: Attach costed workplans with phased implementation

Gap 4: Missing Safety Infrastructure

Current State: No testing standards or evaluation protocols
Risk: Unsafe AI deployments, public trust erosion
Solution: Adopt international standards (ISO/IEC 23053, NIST AI RMF)

Gap 5: Absent Public Procurement Standards

Current State: No AI procurement guidelines for government
Risk: Biased systems, vendor lock-in, opacity
Solution: Issue mandatory AI procurement standard within 90 days

✅ Quick Wins: Actions Achievable Within 90 Days

  1. Draft Risk Classification Regulation - Begin stakeholder consultation
  2. Publish AI Procurement Guidelines - Adapt Singapore’s model
  3. Launch Safety Testing Pilot - Partner with 2-3 institutions
  4. Create AI Incident Registry - Simple online reporting system
  5. Establish Policy Dashboard - Public tracking of targets

💡 Strategic Recommendations for Sustainable Success

Governance Reform

  • Separate AI governance from data protection to avoid mandate confusion
  • Create inter-ministerial coordination committee with clear RACI matrix
  • Establish provincial AI coordination cells for federal alignment

Funding & Resources

  • Secure PKR 5B annual budget for National AI Fund (currently unfunded)
  • Allocate 30% to safety and evaluation infrastructure
  • Create PPP framework for compute infrastructure development

Talent Development

  • Refocus from quantity to quality: Train 500 safety evaluators first
  • Establish AI faculty development program: 100 professors/year
  • Create industry secondment program: Real-world experience

International Alignment

  • Join Partnership on AI and similar multilateral initiatives
  • Adopt ISO/IEC standards for AI management systems
  • Establish bilateral cooperation with Singapore, UK, and Canada

📝 Conclusion: The Path Forward

Pakistan stands at a critical juncture. The National AI Policy provides the vision, but success demands immediate action on enforcement mechanisms, institutional capacity, and safety infrastructure. The next 12 months will determine whether Pakistan becomes an AI leader in South Asia or remains a late adopter.

The choice is clear: Act now with the specific reforms outlined in this brief, or risk permanent disadvantage in the global AI economy.
