The New Reality

AI won’t replace IR analysts.

But IR analysts using AI will replace those who don’t.

Note

Why today? AI augments judgment, scales comparative analysis, and compresses time-to-insight across languages and sources.

Foundations: What is AI?

Core abilities - Learn from data - Recognize patterns - Understand language - Reason over context - Make/advise decisions - Adapt to feedback

Tip

Simple truth: AI processes vast multilingual, multi-source information faster than any person, surfacing patterns in events, treaties, and diplomatic communications.

Generative AI: Why it’s Special for IR

What it can do - Draft memos & talking points - Generate scenarios & options - Adapt style to audience/country - Iterate with feedback - Synthesize large policy corpora

How it works (high level) - Transformers & attention - Pretraining on diverse corpora - Instruction tuning & RAG - Safety alignment & system prompts - Tool-use & function calling

Large Language Models (LLMs)

Power - Global knowledge, multilingual - Long-context reasoning (caveats) - Flexible outputs (text, code, tables) - Transfer & few-shot learning

Leading families - GPT, Claude, Gemini - Open models: Llama, Mixtral, DeepSeek

Building a Simple IR Workflow (RAG)

Flow: Sources → Chunk/Index → Retrieve → LLM → Cite → Human review
- Start with open sources (UN, World Bank, think tanks)
- Maintain a small, vetted corpus - Use retrieval augmentation (RAG) for citations - Document prompts & versions - Keep a review checklist (facts, tone, bias, sensitivity)

💬 Try this Prompt
“Given these 3 URLs (paste), build a 7-bullet brief with inline citations [1][2][3], flag contradictions, and list unknowns to investigate.”

Human + AI: A Division of Labor

AI excels at: breadth, speed, synthesis, multilingual processing
Humans excel at: judgment, context, ethics, accountability, diplomacy

Use AI for drafts; use humans for decisions.

Risks, Limits, and Guardrails

  • Bias in training data → skewed outputs
  • Hallucinations → fabricated facts/citations
  • Misinformation → narrative amplification risks
  • Privacy & security → sensitive sources
  • Over-reliance → deskilling of analytic craft

Warning

Always verify with primary sources; keep an audit trail of prompts and revisions.

💬 Try this Prompt
“List 5 potential harms of using AI to analyze conflict data and propose 5 mitigation steps a university lab can implement.”

Ethics for IR & Academia

  1. Transparency — disclose AI use when appropriate
  2. Attribution — credit sources & collaborators
  3. Verification — cross-check facts; log methods
  4. Privacy — protect vulnerable groups & data
  5. Balance — AI assists; humans decide
💬 Try this Prompt
“Create a 1-page ‘Responsible AI in the Seminar’ policy for undergraduates with bullet points on disclosure, verification, and collaboration norms.”

Teaching with AI: Faculty Notes

  • Scaffold tasks (describe → compare → critique → create)
  • Encourage method transparency (prompt logs)
  • Evaluate process, not just outputs
  • Mix closed-book & oral checks to deter over-reliance
  • Bring country/regional expertise into AI prompts

Quick Reference Prompts (Copy & Use)

  • Briefing: “Summarize [topic] in 6 bullets for a minister, add 2 risks & 2 options.”
  • Compare: “Contrast positions of [A] vs [B] on [issue] with a 5-row table.”
  • Narratives: “Extract 3 narratives and their evidence from these links: [..].”
  • Diplomatic style: “Rewrite this memo in a neutral MFA tone, max 180 words.”
  • Translation check: “Translate to [lang] and back-translate; list divergences.”
  • Scenario: “Draft 3 scenarios (optimistic/base/pessimistic) with signposts.”
