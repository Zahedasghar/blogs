0.1 Introduction — The Significance of a National AI Policy

The introduction of Pakistan’s National AI Policy marks a crucial step in the nation’s technological trajectory. While the draft has notable limitations in enforceability, clarity, and operational detail, the mere act of initiating such a policy reflects a recognition at the highest levels that AI will be central to economic growth, governance efficiency, and societal transformation. In a rapidly evolving global landscape where peers and competitors are already institutionalizing AI governance, Pakistan’s move to formalize its intent — even in draft form — is commendable. The challenge now is to refine this framework into a precise, enforceable, and future-proof instrument that both safeguards public interest and positions Pakistan as a competitive player in the global AI ecosystem.

Document & Method This report applies a 9-step prompt chaining workflow to the uploaded Pakistan National AI Policy — Consultation Draft. Each section starts with the exact prompt followed by the response. A Red Flags table highlights urgent gaps up front. All recommendations include owners and KPIs where possible, and diff-style wording is provided for priority fixes.

1.1 Red Flags — Urgent Gaps (Immediate Attention)

Severe No explicit risk‑based classification of AI systems (minimal articulation of high‑risk vs low‑risk and obligations) Without tiers, you can’t target safeguards or enforcement; generic rules either over‑ or under‑regulate. Adopt a 4‑tier risk scheme (prohibited, high, limited, minimal) with obligations aligned to EU/SG practice.

6 Step 6 — Implementation Feasibility

Prompt Assess governance, regulators, funding, timelines, metrics, talent, PPPs, procurement, and enforcement. Flag dependencies and risks.

Response

  • Governance: Fragmented locus (AI directorate under data‑protection commission) risks mandate confusion. Risk: slow decisions, appeal bottlenecks.
  • Funding: NAIF concept is strong; recurring % allocations need legal clarity and MTBF budgeting. Risk: stop‑go funding.
  • Timelines: Many 12–24m ambitions without lead‑time for rulemaking or capacity build. Risk: systemic deadline slippage.
  • Talent: 1M+ training targets lack sourcing, curriculum, trainer pipeline. Risk: quantity over quality.
  • PPPs & Infra: CoE network viable if capex/opex and utilization plans are published. Risk: stranded assets.
  • Procurement: Absent AI procurement standard leads to opaque buys. Risk: bias, lock‑in, audit failures.
  • Enforcement: No sanction schedule or investigative powers defined. Risk: non‑compliance at scale.

7 Step 7 — Actionable Improvements (with Before/After text)

Prompt Provide concrete edits, new sections to add, governance diagrams, and a 12–24 month roadmap. Include owners and KPIs.

Response

7.0.1 A. Priority Legal/Regulatory Edits

1) Risk Classification — Add new section Before: [No explicit tiers] After (insert): Pakistan shall adopt a four‑tier, risk‑based AI framework (Prohibited, High, Limited, Minimal). High‑risk systems shall undergo conformity assessment, bias and safety evaluations, documentation (model/data cards), and human‑in‑the‑loop controls prior to deployment. Prohibited practices include manipulative/biometric categorization uses defined by regulation. Owner: National AI Authority (NAA) KPI: Tiered rulebook issued in 6 months; 80% of new high‑risk deployments registered in 12 months.

2) Regulator Mandate — Clarify institutional design Before: AI Directorate under PDPA commission with broad/unclear scope. After (replace): Establish the National AI Authority (NAA) as an independent regulator with rulemaking, licensing, investigation, and sanction powers. Execute MoUs with sector regulators (health, finance, transport, education) and PDPA for data/rights alignment. Create a single‑window AI Registry. KPI: NAA established, funded, staffed within 9 months; enforcement policy published within 12 months.

3) Safety & Incident Reporting — Add binding obligations Before: Advisory direction on safety. After (insert): High‑risk systems must complete pre‑deployment evaluation (red‑teaming, robustness, bias tests) and implement post‑deployment monitoring. Report serious incidents within 72 hours to NAA; maintain audit trails for 5 years. KPI: Evaluation protocol published (90 days); first annual incident report by month 18.

4) Public‑Sector AI Procurement — Add standard Before: No standard. After (insert): Publish a Government AI Procurement Standard mandating risk assessment, explainability, human oversight, open auditing interfaces, and a public register of AI systems in use. KPI: 100% of central ministries compliant in 12 months; provinces in 18 months.

5) Generative AI — Operational guardrails Before: Principles only. After (insert): Require provenance/watermarking for synthetic media in public services, disclosure to end‑users, dataset documentation, and a notice‑and‑takedown process for deceptive content. KPI: Compliance guide in 120 days; audits begin in 12 months.

7.0.2 B. Governance & Delivery

  • RACI: NAA (A/R: safety, registry, enforcement), MoITT (A/R: policy & funding), PDPA (C: rights), sector regulators (R: domain rules), provinces (R: implementation), Auditor‑General (I/C: audits).
  • Transparency: Quarterly delivery dashboard; publish consultation logs, sandbox outcomes, and enforcement statistics.

7.0.3 C. KPIs (examples)

  • Rulemaking: 5 priority regulations notified in 12 months.
  • Compliance: 90% of high‑risk systems registered and evaluated pre‑deployment by month 18.
  • Talent: 25k advanced practitioners certified/year; 500 safety evaluators trained.
  • Innovation: 100 sandbox participants → at least 20 scaled deployments with rule updates.

8 Step 8 — Executive Summary (Direct & Critical)

Prompt Synthesize findings into a 1–2 page summary with top 10 priorities and owners.

Response

Bottom line: The draft is comprehensive in ambition but underdelivers on enforceable obligations. Without a risk‑based framework, a clearly empowered regulator, and binding safety/transparency rules, Pakistan will struggle to deploy AI safely at scale and fail to attract quality investment.

Top 10 Priorities (with Owners)

  1. Legislate risk‑based tiers and obligations (NAA/MoITT).
  2. Establish and fund National AI Authority with sanction powers (Parliament/MoITT).
  3. Issue National AI Evaluation Protocol and certify labs (NAA).
  4. Create AI Incident Registry with 72‑hour reporting (NAA).
  5. Publish Govt AI Procurement Standard and system register (Cabinet Division/MoITT).
  6. Convert sandbox learnings into rules on a fixed cadence (NAA + sector regulators).
  7. Tie targets to budgets and delivery owners; publish dashboards (MoITT/Finance).
  8. Build data‑sharing trusts/clean rooms aligned to PDPA (PDPA/NAA).
  9. Refocus talent target to quality + safety roles; train 500 safety evaluators (MoITT/HEC).
  10. Operationalize Generative AI guardrails (provenance, disclosure, redress) (NAA/PDPA).
